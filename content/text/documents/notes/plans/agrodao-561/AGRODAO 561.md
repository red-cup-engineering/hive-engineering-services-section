# AGRODAO 561

***Adapative Generative Resource Operations Decentralized Autonomous Organization for Ending Food Apartheid***, or AGRODAO 561, is a _decentralized autonomous organization_ that uses *adaptive generative* to practice *resource operations* to *end food apartheid*.

- A ***decentralized autonomous organization*** is a group that governs itself using mechanisms designed to avoid centralization and lack of consent, usually implemented through making, discussing, and voting on proposals.
- ***Adaptive generation*** is an iterative approach to work that leverages principles of resilience to adapt to changing conditions.
- ***Resource operations*** is the implementation of governance decisions.
- ***Ending food apartheid*** is the central value that motivates governance and action for the group.

*(Where did "561" come from? E, F, A (End Food Apartheid) are the 5th, 6th, and 1st letters of the English alphabet.)*


AGRODAO is designed to establish a sustainable model of adaptive generative natural resource management on a section of marsh within Minnesota's North Shore. To ensure that AGRODAO remains self-sustaining and financially independent, we will produce and publish multimedia content documenting our practices. This approach not only generates revenue to fund operations but also promotes transparency and encourages community engagement by sharing insights and lessons learned.

## Mission and Vision

### Mission

To establish AGRODAO as a self-sustaining model of regenerative natural resource management that prioritizes ecological health, economic equity, and community empowerment through transparency and decentralized governance.

### Vision

To inspire global adoption of adaptive generative practices that align with Indigenous stewardship principles, fostering a resilient future for communities and ecosystems worldwide.

## Core Values

- **Indigeneity**: Centering Indigenous principles of reciprocity, autonomy, and long-term stewardship in all activities.
    
- **Anarchy**: Upholding non-hierarchical governance structures that emphasize cooperation, mutual aid, and collective decision-making.
    
- **Autonomy**: Supporting both ecological and human systems in achieving self-sufficiency and resilience.
    
- **Transparency**: Ensuring openness in operations, finances, and decision-making to build trust and inspire collaboration.
    
- **Regeneration**: Prioritizing practices that repair and enrich ecosystems, ensuring their vitality for generations to come.

## Stewardship Framework

### Herbalism

- Cultivate medicinal plants native to the local ecosystem or well-adapted to its conditions.
    
- Develop partnerships with local communities to share medicines, creating access for marginalized groups.
    
- Explore value-added products like teas, tinctures, and salves to support AGRODAO’s financial goals.
    

### Forestry

- Implement sustainable, adaptive forestry practices mindful of biodiversity, the water cycle, and climate impacts.
    
- Explore coppicing, agroforestry, and selective harvesting to ensure long-term forest health.
    
- Create biochar and other soil amendments as by-products of sustainable forestry efforts.
    

### Horticulture

- Focus on specimen banking, including seed preservation, rootstock cultivation, and propagation of resilient species.
    
- Maintain and enhance biodiversity to create a robust genetic pool for future generations.
    
- Collaborate with other land stewards and organizations to share genetic resources and knowledge.
    

### Hydrology

- Develop strategies to manage marshland water resources sustainably, ensuring minimal disruption to local ecosystems.
    
- Monitor and adapt to climate-related changes in water levels and quality.
    
- Integrate water stewardship practices into all aspects of AGRODAO’s operations.
